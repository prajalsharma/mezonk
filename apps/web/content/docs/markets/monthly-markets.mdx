You don't have to wait for someone to suggest a market. Once a month MEZONK lists a fresh set on its own:

- **Bitcoin price markets**, like *Will Bitcoin be at or above $120,000 on Mezo's price feed at 17:00 UTC, Nov 30,
  2026?* They settle on the BTC price Mezo itself uses, with no person deciding.
- **Two picks beyond Bitcoin**, drawn at random from the questions people are trading most right now: an interest-rate
  decision, an economic number, a sports final. They settle from the official source named in their rules.

Both kinds go through the same review as every other market and start with orders on both sides.

## The plain version

- **Chosen by what's active.** The Bitcoin markets are the most traded Bitcoin price questions of the moment. The two
  other picks are drawn at random from the 20 most traded open questions that pass the checks below.
- **They start with real orders.** A MEZONK wallet places a Yes bid and a No bid about 6¢ apart, around the price the
  question trades at elsewhere, with its own MUSD. Anyone can trade against them.
- **They may wait in seeding.** If the market policy needs more than one seeder, a market stays in *Seeding* until
  someone else adds orders. You can be that seeder: see [Seeding and launch](/docs/how-it-works/seeding-and-launch).
- **They settle on their own.** Bitcoin markets are read from Mezo's price feed right after close. For the other picks,
  the resolver proposes the result from the named source once it's out. Either way you can challenge it, and claims
  open once it's final.

## Which questions qualify

**Bitcoin markets** must be about the price at one moment. Mezo's price feed has no history: it can be read now, not
looked up later. So the resolver reads it once, just after close, and that answers exactly one question: was BTC at
or above the strike then?

| Listed | Not listed |
|---|---|
| "Bitcoin at or above $120,000 at 17:00 UTC on Nov 30?" | "Will Bitcoin reach $130,000 in November?" (any time during a month) |
| "Bitcoin below $90,000 at 22:00 UTC on Dec 15?" | Price ranges like "$121,000 to $121,499" |
| | "Bitcoin up or down today?" (that's [BTC up or down](/docs/mezo/btc-up-down)) |

**Picks beyond Bitcoin** must have rules that work on their own:

- a clear "Yes if …" condition;
- an official https source that publishes the result (a central bank, a statistics office, a league);
- no crypto (Bitcoin has its own markets), and nothing the [market policy](/docs/markets/market-policy) forbids;
- they pass the same automatic rule checks as a suggestion.

For both kinds: Yes between 10% and 90% (nobody trades a sure thing), and an end date between one day after seeding
and 45 days out.

## How it works exactly

Each month the listing job:

1. Reads the most traded open questions, keeps the ones that qualify, and picks the Bitcoin markets by volume and the
   other two at random.
2. Writes MEZONK rules. Bitcoin markets use a fixed template: strike, "at or above" or "below", the close time in
   UTC, Mezo's PriceFeed as the source, and Invalid only if the feed publishes nothing for 24 hours after close. The
   other picks keep the original condition and name its official source, with Invalid if that source publishes no
   result within 7 days.
3. Suggests each one from the listing wallet. A curator wallet reviews it, assigns the resolver and signs the
   approval. The listing wallet publishes it and seeds both sides; Bitcoin markets also get their strike recorded on
   the price resolver.

A daily run launches markets whose seeding is complete and proposes outcomes for ended picks. The keeper bot
finalizes any market whose deadline has passed, so claims open without anyone having to press *Finalize*.

Every step leaves the usual public trail: the proposal page shows the review and the approval, the rules hash is
on-chain, and every proposal carries its evidence.

<Callout type="info" title="Automated wallets">
  The listing, curator and resolver wallets used for monthly markets are keys run by a server. The curator signs
  only rules that passed the checks above, and the factory owner can remove its role at any time. People reviewing
  community suggestions use separate wallets.
</Callout>
