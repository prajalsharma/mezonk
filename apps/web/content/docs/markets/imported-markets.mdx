Once a month MEZONK looks at the most traded Bitcoin price questions on Polymarket and Kalshi and lists the same
questions here. They go through the same review as any other market, settle on the BTC price Mezo itself uses, and
start with orders on both sides so you can trade them as soon as they launch.

## The plain version

- **Same question, Mezo's price.** "Will Bitcoin be at or above $120,000 at 17:00 UTC on Nov 30?" is answered by
  reading Mezo's BTC price right after that moment, not the price Polymarket or Kalshi uses.
- **The page shows the other market.** Under the question you'll see *Same question on Polymarket* (or Kalshi), with
  its current Yes price and when it was read. That's for comparison only: it doesn't set the price here and doesn't
  decide the result.
- **The two can disagree at the edge.** Mezo's price and an exchange price can differ by a few dollars. If Bitcoin ends
  right at the strike, the two markets can resolve differently. Read the rules on the market page.
- **It starts with real orders.** The importer places a Yes bid and a No bid about 6¢ apart around the other market's
  price, with its own MUSD. They're ordinary orders that anyone can trade against.
- **It may wait in seeding.** If the market policy needs more than one seeder, the market stays in *Seeding* until
  someone else adds orders. You can be that seeder: see [Seeding and launch](/docs/how-it-works/seeding-and-launch).

## Which questions qualify

Only questions about the price at **one moment**. Mezo's price feed has no history: it can be read now, not
looked up later. So the resolver reads it once, just after close, and that reading answers exactly one question:
was BTC at or above the strike then?

| Imported | Not imported |
|---|---|
| "Bitcoin above $120,000 on November 30?" | "Will Bitcoin reach $130,000 in November?" (any time during a month) |
| Kalshi's "$100,000 or above" at 5pm on a date | Price ranges like "$121,000 to $121,499" |
| | "Bitcoin up or down today?" (that's [BTC up or down](/docs/mezo/btc-up-down)) |
| | Questions already at 90% or 10% (nobody trades them) |

## How it works exactly

Each month the importer:

1. Reads open Bitcoin markets from Polymarket's and Kalshi's public APIs.
2. Keeps the point-in-time above/below questions measured between one day after seeding ends and 45 days out,
   with Yes between 10% and 90%. The same moment and strike on both sources counts once.
3. Ranks them by 24-hour volume and takes the top three, at most two per moment.
4. Writes MEZONK rules from a fixed template: the strike, "at or above" or "below", the close time in UTC, Mezo's
   PriceFeed as the source, and Invalid only if the feed publishes no price for 24 hours after close.
5. Suggests each one from the import creator wallet. The import curator wallet reviews it, assigns the price
   resolver and signs the approval. The creator publishes it, the curator records the strike on the resolver, and the
   creator seeds both sides.

Every step leaves the usual public trail: the proposal page shows the source market, the review and the approval;
the rules hash is on-chain; the strike is in the resolver's `RuleSet` event.

After close, the keeper calls `settle()`, which reads Mezo's PriceFeed and proposes Yes or No with the exact reading
as evidence. The challenge window and arbitration work as for every market. See
[BTC price markets](/docs/mezo/btc-price-markets) for the resolver's checks.

<Callout type="info" title="An automated curator">
  The import curator is a wallet whose key runs on a server. It signs only rules the importer generates from the
  template above, and the factory owner can remove its curator role at any time. It's a separate wallet from the
  people who review community suggestions.
</Callout>
