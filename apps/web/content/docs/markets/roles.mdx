A MEZONK market passes through several hands: someone suggests it, a curator approves it, a creator publishes it, seeders fund it, traders trade it, a resolver proposes the result and an arbitrator settles disputes. Most of these are open to anyone. A few are appointed, and their powers are deliberately narrow: nobody who can publish a market can also approve it, and nobody in the app decides outcomes.

## The short version

- **Open to anyone:** suggesting, seeding, trading, referring, challenging a proposed outcome, finalizing once a deadline passes, and claiming.
- **Appointed on-chain by the owner:** curators (approve markets), approved creators (publish them), resolvers (propose outcomes). The arbitrator is set in the configuration copied into each new market.
- **Appointed in MEZONK's database:** administrators, who run the website's moderation and send role changes to the owner for signing. They have no on-chain power.
- **The owner** sets the rules for future markets and can pause new activity, but can't move your MUSD, change an outcome, or block anyone from getting their money out.

## Every role

| Role | How it's granted | Can | Can't |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visitor | Nobody: anyone with a browser | Read markets, rules, evidence, resolution history, suggestions and review decisions | Sign anything |
| Member | Sign in with a wallet (one free signature) | Suggest markets, comment, upload images, edit a profile | Approve or publish anything |
| Verified project representative | Claims a project; a curator verifies the public proof | Everything a member can, plus suggest markets about their own project with a visible disclosure | Approve their own market, decide an outcome. Verification is a label, not a protocol power |
| Seeder | Places a seed order on a seeding market | Fund a market's first orders; share fee rebates after launch | Launch a market alone if the policy needs more seeders; cancel during the lock |
| Trader | Anyone with MUSD | Buy, sell, place and cancel orders, split, merge, claim | Trade against their own order |
| Referrer | Named by another wallet before its first trade | Earn a capped share of that wallet's taker fees | Earn on fills where they were the maker; earn from two levels down |
| Curator | Owner calls `factory.setCurator(wallet, true)` | Review suggestions, sign approvals, void unused approvals, verify project representatives, moderate comments | Publish a market they approved (reverts on-chain); review their own suggestion (refused by the backend); edit a suggestion's content; touch outcomes |
| Approved creator (publisher) | Owner calls `factory.setCreator(wallet, true)` | Publish a market with a valid, unused curator approval that names them | Publish anything unapproved; change approved rules; publish an approval they signed themselves |
| Resolver | Owner calls `factory.setResolver(wallet, true)`; a curator assigns one per market | Propose the outcome after close, with evidence and a bond | Challenge proposals; change a proposal; finalize early |
| Challenger | Anyone except the market's resolver and arbitrator | Challenge a proposal once, with a bond and evidence | Challenge after the window or a second time |
| Arbitrator | Set in the factory configuration; copied into each market at publication | Rule Yes, No or Invalid on a challenged market before its deadline, with a published rationale | Act on an unchallenged market; act late; change a final outcome |
| Finalizer | Anyone | Finalize once a deadline condition holds | Finalize early |
| Administrator | Database role, granted by another administrator in the admin area | Moderate content, suspend accounts, verify projects, read the audit log, queue on-chain role and policy changes for the owner to sign | Grant on-chain roles themselves; review or publish markets; move funds; decide outcomes |
| Owner | `Ownable2Step` owner of the exchange and the factory | Add or retire policies, set configuration for future markets, grant and revoke curators, creators and resolvers, pause and unpause, withdraw the protocol's fee share | See the next section |

One wallet can hold several roles. The rules that stop conflicts are listed below.

## What the owner can't do

The exchange and the factory each have an owner (the deploy script hands both to the same address). The owner's powers are limited by the code, not by promise:

- **Can't move user money.** Collateral backing shares, MUSD and shares locked in orders, rebate and referral reserves, and bonds are all out of reach. `withdrawProtocolFees` sends only the protocol's accrued fee share.
- **Can't change an outcome.** Only `MezonkResolution` can settle a market, and the owner has no function there that picks an outcome.
- **Can't change a published market.** Rules, policy, resolver, arbitrator, windows and bonds are fixed in each market at publication. New configuration and new policies apply to future markets only.
- **Can't block exits.** Pause stops publishing, new orders, takes, splits and launches. It never stops cancelling orders, merging, refunds, resolution, claims or bond withdrawals.
- **Can't upgrade the contracts.** There is no upgrade mechanism. A change means a new deployment, and existing markets keep settling on the old one.

What the owner **can** do still matters, and you should know it:

- Appoint the curators and creators, so publication is only as independent as the people the owner picks. An owner who controlled both a curator and a creator wallet could publish any market.
- Choose the arbitrator for future markets, and the arbitrator decides every challenged market.
- Pause the exchange. A long pause can make seeding markets fail (seeders are still refunded) and stops trading.

On Mezo testnet today, the owner and the arbitrator are single-key wallets, not multisigs (checked on 6 November 2026). The intended setup before any mainnet use is a multisig for each, held by different people.

## How on-chain roles are granted

Creator, curator and resolver are lists in `MezonkMarketFactory`. The owner changes them with `setCreator`, `setCurator` and `setResolver`, and each change emits an event (`CreatorUpdated`, `CuratorUpdated`, `ResolverUpdated`).

Administrators manage this from the admin area: they pick a wallet and press grant or revoke, which records an operation with the exact transaction data. The owner then signs it from their wallet, or proposes it from a Safe. The app shows it as confirmed only after it finds the successful transaction with the matching event and a fresh contract read shows the new value. There is no second, weaker list in the database for these roles: the chain is the only authority.

Ownership itself moves with `Ownable2Step`: the current owner nominates a new one, who must call `acceptOwnership()` on each contract.

## When a role changes

Roles are checked at the moment they're used, and each market copies what it needs at publication. So:

| Change | Effect on new markets | Effect on markets already published |
|---|---|---|
| Curator revoked | Can't approve | Their unused approvals stop working (checked at publication) |
| Creator revoked | Can't publish | None: a creator has no powers after publishing |
| Resolver revoked | Can't be assigned | None: the market keeps its resolver, who can still propose |
| New arbitrator in config | Used for markets published afterwards | None: each market keeps the arbitrator it was published with |
| Policy retired | Can't be used for new approvals | None: markets keep their policy |

That last column is deliberate: nobody can retarget an open market's resolution by changing a setting.

## Separation rules

| Rule | Where it's enforced |
|---|---|
| A curator can't publish a market they approved | Contract (`SelfApproval`) |
| A curator can't review their own suggestion | Backend |
| The resolver and arbitrator can't challenge | Contract |
| The resolver and arbitrator are different wallets | Deploy script, at setup only. The contract doesn't check it afterwards |
| The first curator differs from the resolver, the arbitrator and the first creator | Deploy script, at setup only |
| An administrator can't revoke themselves or the last administrator | Backend |

## Backend permissions

The website's own permissions (who can comment, moderate, upload, review in the app) are computed on the server from these facts on every request. A role, wallet or author id sent from the browser is never trusted. Curator and creator come from the chain; administrator and suspension come from the database. A suspended account loses every capability and all its sessions at once. The app never holds a privileged key.
