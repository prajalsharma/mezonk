Solvency means the contracts can always pay what they owe. MEZONK gets there with one rule: a Yes share and a No
share are only ever created together, from exactly 1 MUSD that the exchange keeps until the market ends. Whichever
side wins, there is exactly 1 MUSD behind each winning share (or 0.5 MUSD behind every share if the market is
Invalid). There is no house position, no loan to the protocol and no reward paid from anything but fees.

## The invariants

| # | Invariant | Why it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Before a market is final, its Yes supply = its No supply = its collateral | Shares are minted only by `split` or by a fill that pairs two opposite buyers whose prices add to 100¢; burned only by `merge`, a pairing fill, or `redeem` |
| 2 | After it's final, its collateral = what the remaining shares redeem for | `redeem` pays exactly 1, 0 or 0.5 per share and subtracts that from the market's collateral |
| 3 | Exchange balance = `totalCollateral + escrowedCollateral + protocolFees` | Every transfer in or out updates exactly one bucket |
| 4 | Escrow = the sum of open resting buy orders' `remaining × price / 100` | Placing, filling and cancelling each move escrow by the exact cost |
| 5 | Incentives balance = `rebateReserve + referralReserve` | Rewards are funded only by the fee share the exchange sends inside `take` |
| 6 | Rebates claimed ≤ rebates accrued, per market | Pro-rata entitlement capped by what accrued, then per wallet |
| 7 | Resolution balance = `lockedBonds + bondCreditsTotal` (next deployment: `+ slashedBonds`) | Bonds are pulled, credited and withdrawn through one ledger |
| 8 | Nothing rounds | Prices are whole cents and quantities multiples of 0.01 share, so `q × p / 100` is exact |

Fees are never part of collateral, refundable seed escrow is never revenue, and rebate and referral payouts can never
exceed what fees funded (rebate plus referral shares are capped at 80% of the fee).

## How they're tested

In `contracts/test/exchange/ExchangeInvariants.t.sol`:

- **Stateful invariant tests** (`ExchangeInvariantTest`): `invariant_bucketsEqualBalance`,
  `invariant_sharesFullyBacked`, `invariant_escrowMatchesOpenOrders` and `invariant_rebatesWithinAccrued` run after
  every call of a handler that places, cancels, takes resting and signed orders, splits, merges, transfers, graduates,
  warps time, resolves, redeems and claims rebates. The file configures 64 runs at depth 200; the `ci` profile in
  `foundry.toml` raises the default to 256 runs at depth 128.
- **Random walks** (`ExchangeRandomWalkTest.testFuzz_randomWalk`): 300 seeded random actions per run with every
  invariant checked every 25 steps and at the end, and assertions that the walk actually reached resting fills,
  signed fills, placements and splits or merges. 40 runs by default, 200 under `ci`.
- **Value conservation** (`testFuzz_fillConservesValue`): for any price and size, the two sides of a pairing fill add
  up to exactly one set, and the fee is exact.
- **Fork tests** (`contracts/test/fork/`) run a full lifecycle against the real testnet MUSD contract.

Run them with `cd contracts && forge test`, or at CI depth with
`FOUNDRY_PROFILE=ci forge test --match-contract "ExchangeRandomWalkTest|ExchangeInvariantTest"`.

## Check the live contracts yourself

You need [Foundry's `cast`](https://book.getfoundry.sh). These are read-only calls against Mezo testnet.

```bash
RPC=https://rpc.test.mezo.org
MUSD=0x118917a40FAF1CD7a13dB0Ef56C86De7973Ac503
EX=0x4555cfF10200e1007435Cffc683C60683c5C49B9
RES=0x6Da57dDd874f02E3ad704F75aCC86D0319d3b944
INC=0xF1a014E4E291EB2690777104D65990efAFC3f06B
```

### Exchange: balance equals its three buckets

```bash
cast call $MUSD "balanceOf(address)(uint256)" $EX --rpc-url $RPC
cast call $EX "totalCollateral()(uint256)"    --rpc-url $RPC
cast call $EX "escrowedCollateral()(uint256)" --rpc-url $RPC
cast call $EX "protocolFees()(uint256)"       --rpc-url $RPC
```

The first number should equal the sum of the other three. It can only be larger if someone sent MUSD to the exchange
directly, which nothing in MEZONK does. Read all four at the same block (add `--block <n>`) if trades are happening.

### Incentives and resolution

```bash
cast call $MUSD "balanceOf(address)(uint256)" $INC --rpc-url $RPC
cast call $INC "rebateReserve()(uint256)"   --rpc-url $RPC
cast call $INC "referralReserve()(uint256)" --rpc-url $RPC

cast call $MUSD "balanceOf(address)(uint256)" $RES --rpc-url $RPC
cast call $RES "lockedBonds()(uint256)"      --rpc-url $RPC
cast call $RES "bondCreditsTotal()(uint256)" --rpc-url $RPC
```

### One market

```bash
cast call $EX "marketCount()(uint256)" --rpc-url $RPC
cast call $EX "stageOf(uint256)(uint8)" 1 --rpc-url $RPC      # 0 seeding, 1 live, 2 failed, 3 closed, 4 final
cast call $EX "sharesOf(uint256,uint8,address)(uint256)" 1 0 <wallet> --rpc-url $RPC   # Yes shares
cast call $EX "sharesOf(uint256,uint8,address)(uint256)" 1 1 <wallet> --rpc-url $RPC   # No shares
```

`getMarket(id)` returns the whole market struct, including `collateral`. The sum of every market's collateral is
`totalCollateral`. Total share supply isn't stored on-chain (shares are a mapping), so checking invariant 1 for a
market means summing `Split`, `Fill`, `Merged`, `Redeemed` and `OrderPlaced`/`OrderCancelled` sell escrow from its
events; the indexer does exactly this and reconciles collateral against `getMarket` every five minutes.

## What solvency doesn't cover

Solvency says the money is there; it doesn't say the result is right. A wrong final outcome pays the wrong side in
full. That risk sits with resolution and the arbitrator: see [Trust model](/docs/security/trust-model) and
[Risks](/docs/security/risks). It also assumes MUSD behaves like a standard ERC-20, which MEZONK checks on every
transfer in.
