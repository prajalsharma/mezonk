MEZONK's contracts are not upgradeable, and none of them has a function that lets anyone take the MUSD that backs
shares, sits in order escrow, funds rebates or is posted as a bond. What people with special roles can do is limited
to settings for future markets, pausing new activity, and (for the arbitrator) ruling on challenged results. The
biggest single point of trust is the arbitrator. MEZONK is **not independently audited**; see
[Audits](/docs/security/audits).

## Who can do what

| Role | Can | Cannot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exchange owner | Add graduation and fee policies for new markets; retire a policy for new markets; pause and unpause; withdraw accrued protocol fees | Move collateral, escrow, rebate or referral reserves, or bonds; change any outcome; change an existing market's policy; block exits |
| Factory owner (same key today) | Add or remove creators, curators and resolvers; set windows, bonds and arbitrator for markets published afterwards; void an unused approval | Change those settings for markets already published; publish a market without a curator's signature |
| Arbitrator | Rule on a challenged result within the arbitration window, choosing Yes, No or Invalid, with a rationale hash | Act on unchallenged markets; challenge a result; rule after the window (it then settles Invalid with bonds refunded) |
| Curator | Sign an approval for one exact market (rules hash, creator, resolver, close time, policy); void an approval | Publish the market they approved; make a market tradable (only funded seed orders can); touch funds or outcomes |
| Creator | Publish a market a curator approved | Publish without an approval, change its rules, or reuse an approval |
| Resolver | Propose a result with a bond after close | Finalize without the challenge window passing; challenge its own proposal from the same address |
| Anyone | Trade; challenge a proposal with a bond; finalize after a deadline; graduate a market that meets its policy | |
| MEZONK server | Store signed orders and suggestions; serve indexed data | Sign for anyone, hold keys, invent fills or prices, authorize anything financial |

The exchange and factory both use `Ownable2Step`: a new owner must accept before control moves. On testnet the owner
is a single key (`0x017FF5FB886E90f9e5ADb405D8eFddffC1162dD2`, an EOA), not a multisig.

## What pausing does

`pause()` stops new risk: publishing markets, graduation, splits, placing orders and taking. It never stops
cancelling orders, merging, transferring shares, proposing, challenging, arbitrating, finalizing, redeeming or
claiming rebates and bonds. A paused exchange can't trap money, but it can make a market in seeding miss its
deadline (it then fails and every seeder can cancel for a full refund) or stop trading on a live market until
unpaused.

## How it works exactly

### The owner has no path to user funds

Every MUSD the exchange holds is in exactly one bucket: `totalCollateral` (backs shares), `escrowedCollateral`
(open resting buy orders) or `protocolFees`. The only owner function that sends MUSD is
`withdrawProtocolFees(to)`, which sends `protocolFees` and nothing else. The incentives contract has no owner. The
resolution contract has no owner; on the next deployment the exchange owner can sweep only `slashedBonds`. Fuzzed
invariants check that the buckets always equal the token balances: see [Solvency](/docs/security/solvency).

### Outcomes come from one place

`MezonkExchange.settle` reverts unless `msg.sender` is the resolution contract the exchange created, and only once,
only for a market that graduated and closed. The resolution contract finalizes in exactly four ways: an undisputed
proposal after the challenge window, an arbitrator ruling, no proposal by the end of the proposal window (Invalid), or
no ruling by the end of the arbitration window (Invalid, both bonds refunded).

| Situation (live contract) | Who gets the bonds |
| --- | --- |
| Undisputed | Resolver gets its bond back |
| Disputed, arbitrator agrees with the proposal | Resolver gets both bonds |
| Disputed, arbitrator overturns it | Disputer gets both bonds |
| Arbitrator misses the window | Both refunded; market settles Invalid |

### Per-market snapshots

When a market is published, the factory's current config (windows, bonds, arbitrator) and the chosen policy (fees,
seeding rules, incentive caps) are copied into it. A later `setConfig` or `addPolicy` only affects new markets. The
flip side: the owner can't replace the arbitrator on markets that already exist, even if that key is lost.

### Separation checks

- On-chain: the curator can't be the publisher (`SelfApproval`); the resolver and arbitrator can't dispute.
- The deploy script refuses an arbitrator equal to the resolver. The role setters themselves don't re-check this, so
  keeping roles apart after deployment is an owner responsibility.
- An approval nonce can be used once; approvals expire at their `deadline`.

### What changes in the next deployment

<Callout type="warn" title="Next deployment">
  From commit c8fbaff, not on testnet yet: after half the proposal window anyone can propose, so a silent resolver
  can't force Invalid; the proposer, whoever it is, can't dispute their own proposal; an overturned proposal loses half
  its bond to the protocol, so disputing your own false proposal from a second wallet costs money. Seeder rebates count
  only seed escrow inside the graduation band.
</Callout>

### Off-chain roles

Administrators (a database role) manage accounts, comments, media and the review queue, and prepare owner
transactions for the owner to sign. They can't sign on-chain actions, change roles on-chain, or touch funds. The
server reads creator and curator roles from the factory on every privileged request; a database role can never grant
an on-chain power.
