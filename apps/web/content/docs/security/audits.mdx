**MEZONK has not been audited by an independent security firm.** Passing tests and internal reviews are not an
audit. The contracts run on Mezo testnet with test money only, and they should not go to mainnet before an external
review of the contracts and their economics.

What has happened so far: an internal, tool-assisted review of the first (AMM) version of the contracts, and a report
from an external security researcher, KupiaSec, on the current order-book contracts. Every finding below has a fix
in the code. Some fixes are live on testnet; the rest are merged on GitHub and wait for the next deployment.

## Status at a glance

| Finding | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A curator couldn't cancel an approval it had signed | Internal review | Fixed, live (`cancelApproval` on the factory) |
| The arbitrator could challenge a proposal and then rule on its own challenge | Internal review | Fixed, live (the arbitrator can't dispute) |
| A resolver could challenge its own wrong proposal from a second wallet and keep its bond | Internal review | Fixed in commit c8fbaff, not deployed |
| A silent resolver could force Invalid (0.5 per share) by never proposing | Internal review | Fixed in commit c8fbaff, not deployed |
| A view function reverted after liquidity was withdrawn | Internal review | Applies only to the retired AMM; the order book has no such function |
| Far-off seed orders could dilute seeders' rebates | KupiaSec | Fixed in commit c8fbaff, not deployed |

<Callout type="warn" title="Next deployment">
  Commit c8fbaff changes three contracts: rebate weight comes only from seed escrow inside the graduation band; anyone
  may propose after half the proposal window; an overturned proposal loses half its bond to the protocol; the
  proposer can't dispute their own proposal. The contracts aren't upgradeable, so these need a new exchange
  deployment. Until then, the live testnet contracts behave as before.
</Callout>

## How it works exactly

### Internal review, 2026-10-20

`docs/AUDIT_2026-10-20.md` in the repo. An AI-assisted Solidity review (three passes) of the v1 AMM contract
`MezonkMarkets` at commit 4226d80, before the order book existed. It reported five findings and a list of leads.
The resolution logic it reviewed carried over to `MezonkResolution`, so findings 2 to 4 apply to the current
contracts too:

1. **Curator can't cancel an approval.** The factory's `cancelApproval(nonce)` lets a curator or the owner void an
   unused approval.
2. **Arbitrator disputes and rules.** `dispute` rejects the market's resolver and arbitrator.
3. **Self-dispute from a second wallet.** In c8fbaff, an overturned proposal loses half the proposer bond
   (`slashedBonds`), so a resolver disputing its own wrong proposal from another wallet loses money. The proposer
   address is also barred from disputing.
4. **Silent resolver.** In c8fbaff, the resolver has the first half of the proposal window to itself; after that
   anyone may propose with the same bond.
5. **`priceYes` divide by zero.** AMM only.

Several leads remain design limits rather than bugs, and are listed honestly on [Risks](/docs/security/risks): a
single arbitrator, fixed bonds that don't scale with market size, role separation enforced only by the deploy script,
no way to replace the arbitrator on existing markets.

### KupiaSec report

An external researcher reported that seeder rebates were split by all escrow placed during seeding, including orders
priced far from the market (for example Yes at 1¢) that take no real fill risk. A large far-off order, cancelled
after the lock, could dilute honest seeders' rebates. The fix in c8fbaff tracks seed escrow per price level, fixes
the band that graduation measured at the moment of graduation, and counts only escrow inside it
(`rebateWeight`, `totalRebateWeight`). The same commit also carries fixes 3 and 4 above.

Tests for these fixes are in `contracts/test/exchange/AuditFixes.t.sol`: far-off seed escrow earns no rebate weight,
cancelling after launch keeps only in-band weight, no weight before graduation, anyone proposes after half the
window, the proposer can't dispute its own proposal, and a self-dispute from a second wallet loses money.

### What an independent audit should cover

- `MezonkExchange`, `MezonkMarketFactory`, `MezonkResolution`, `MezonkIncentives` and `MezonkPriceResolver` as of
  the next deployment.
- The economics: seeding and graduation, rebate and referral incentives, bond sizes against market size.
- The arbitrator's role and the path to a multisig or decentralized arbitration.
