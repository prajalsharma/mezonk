MEZONK runs on Mezo testnet with test money only. This page lists what can still go wrong, including the things the
design accepts on purpose. If you build on MEZONK or would one day put real money in it, read this first.

## The main ones

- **It isn't independently audited.** Internal reviews and an external researcher's report have been addressed, but no
  audit firm has reviewed these contracts or their economics. See [Audits](/docs/security/audits).
- **One arbitrator decides challenged results.** If that key rules wrongly, the wrong side is paid in full.
- **You can lose what you put in.** A share on the losing side pays nothing.
- **A Mezo loan can be liquidated.** If BTC falls far enough, you lose the BTC you locked.

## Resolution

| Risk | What limits it | What's left |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A resolver proposes a wrong result | Anyone can challenge with a bond during the challenge window; the arbitrator rules | Needs at least one attentive, funded challenger in time |
| The arbitrator rules wrongly or is captured | Rationale hash is public; deadline-bounded; snapshotted per market | Centralized. One address, not a multisig or a court |
| A resolver stays silent to force Invalid (0.5 per share) on the live contract | Nothing on the live contract | Fixed in the next deployment: anyone may propose after half the window |
| A resolver challenges its own wrong proposal from a second wallet on the live contract | The arbitrator can still rule correctly | Fixed in the next deployment: an overturned proposal loses half its bond |
| Someone challenges a correct result just to delay payouts | They lose their bond if the arbitrator rules | Payouts can wait up to the arbitration window |
| The arbitrator misses its window | Market settles Invalid, both bonds refunded | Holders of the right side get 0.5 instead of 1 |
| Bonds are fixed, not scaled to market size | Curator review of who resolves which market | On a large market a 25 MUSD bond (today's testnet setting) is small next to what a wrong result could pay |
| A market's answer becomes known before trading closes | Curators review close times against the event | Not enforceable on-chain for arbitrary events |
| Evidence links change after they're posted | The document hash is committed on-chain and re-checked | External web pages can change; archived links are better |

## Owner and keys

- **The owner is one key on testnet,** not a multisig. It can pause new activity, add or retire policies, change the
  arbitrator and windows for future markets, and allowlist resolvers. It can't touch user funds or outcomes.
- **The owner can give one wallet several roles.** The deploy script refuses resolver = arbitrator, but the role
  setters don't re-check, so separation after deployment depends on the owner.
- **A lost arbitrator key can't be replaced on existing markets,** because each market snapshots its arbitrator.
  Disputes on those markets would time out to Invalid.
- **Pausing during seeding** can make a market miss its deadline. It then fails and every seeder can cancel for a
  full refund, so money isn't lost, but the market doesn't launch.

## Trading

- **Signed orders can go stale.** A maker can spend the MUSD or shares behind a signed order. The contract skips such
  orders instead of failing the whole trade, and the API re-checks funding before quoting, so the effect is a smaller
  fill, never a worse price than your limit.
- **Seeder rebates reward launch liquidity,** not liquidity over time. On the live contract, seed orders far from the
  price also count toward rebate weight; the next deployment counts only escrow inside the graduation band (reported
  by KupiaSec).
- **Wallets aren't people.** The seeding policy's "distinct seeders" counts funded wallets, and one person can control
  several.
- **API rate limits are per server instance** and in memory. Production needs a shared limiter in front.

## Mezo loans

- **Liquidation:** under 110%, anyone can liquidate your loan on Mezo and you lose the locked BTC.
- **Recovery mode:** if Mezo's whole system falls under 150%, you can't borrow more, take BTC out or close until it
  recovers. You can still repay and add BTC.
- **Redemptions:** someone can swap MUSD for BTC from the lowest-ratio loans first. You keep the dollar value but hold
  less BTC.
- **Repaying from winnings takes a second signature.** If you skip it or it fails, the winnings stay in your wallet
  and the loan is unchanged.
- **Mezo's own contracts and price feed** are outside MEZONK's control. MEZONK verifies their addresses and reads
  their parameters live, but doesn't audit them.

## BTC price markets (not deployed yet)

- **No price history.** Mezo's oracle only answers the latest price. If nobody settles within the capture window,
  the result must be proposed by hand.
- **"At close" means "just after close".** The reading is the first fresh price after close, usually within a minute
  or two, not the price at the exact second.
- **The resolver's owner** records the rule and handles the manual fallback, and can sweep the MUSD the resolver
  uses for bonds.

## Operations

- **Deploy gas margin:** the exchange deployment uses about 9.05M of Mezo's 10M block gas. Growing the contract
  could make it undeployable in one transaction.
- **Testnet RPC:** one node behind Mezo testnet's public endpoint lags far behind. The app and scripts work around
  it; other tools may see stale data.
- **The indexer is a copy.** If it's behind or wrong, lists and history are, too. Money views read the chain.
- **Legal review** of operating a prediction market is on the list of steps before mainnet. None is recorded yet.
