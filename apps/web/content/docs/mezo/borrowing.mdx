If you hold BTC but no MUSD, you can borrow MUSD against your BTC on the *Borrow* page without leaving MEZONK. It is
a loan from Mezo's own contracts, not from MEZONK: your wallet sends the BTC to Mezo, Mezo mints MUSD to your wallet,
and your BTC stays locked there until you repay. MEZONK never holds the BTC or the loan.

## The plain version

- **You lock BTC and receive MUSD.** Mezo calls a loan a *trove*. One wallet can have one open loan.
- **Your BTC is not sold.** If BTC goes up, it is still your BTC once you repay.
- **There's a one-time fee and a small yearly interest rate,** both set by Mezo and shown live on the form. There is
  no due date.
- **Mezo adds a refundable 200 MUSD deposit to what you owe.** You get it back when you close the loan.
- **Keep a buffer.** Your *collateral ratio* is the value of your BTC divided by what you owe. If it falls below
  110% because BTC dropped, Mezo can liquidate the loan and you lose the locked BTC. The app suggests 200%.

## Open a loan

<Steps>
<Step>
### Enter the BTC to lock

On the *Borrow* page, type an amount under *BTC to lock*, or tap *Max*. *Max* keeps 0.001 BTC back for network
fees, because gas on Mezo is paid in BTC.
</Step>
<Step>
### Pick a buffer

*How much buffer?* offers *Careful* (250%), *Balanced* (200%) and *Bold* (160%). Picking one fills in the largest
amount of MUSD you can receive at that ratio. You can also type the amount under *MUSD you receive* yourself.
</Step>
<Step>
### Check the summary

The summary shows *You lock*, *You receive*, *One-time fee*, *Refundable deposit*, *Total you owe*, *Interest*, the
ratio meter with the 110% line, and *Liquidated if BTC falls to*. A warning appears if the ratio is under 130%.
</Step>
<Step>
### Confirm and sign

Tick the box that says you understand the loan comes from Mezo, not MEZONK, then press *Borrow … MUSD*. Your wallet
signs one transaction. When it confirms, the MUSD is in your wallet.
</Step>
</Steps>

## A worked example

This uses the parameters Mezo testnet reported on 2026-11-06 (fee 0.1%, deposit 200 MUSD, minimum net debt 1,800
MUSD, 1% interest a year) and assumes BTC at $80,000 to keep the arithmetic round. The form always reads the real
values at the moment you sign.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| You receive | 2,000 MUSD |
| One-time fee (0.1%) | 2 MUSD |
| Refundable deposit | 200 MUSD |
| Total you owe | 2,202 MUSD |
| BTC to lock for 200% | 2,202 × 2 ÷ 80,000 = 0.05505 BTC |
| Liquidated if BTC falls to | 2,202 × 1.10 ÷ 0.05505 = $44,000 |

The smallest loan is set by the minimum net debt. Mezo counts the amount you receive plus the fee, so with a 0.1% fee
the smallest amount you can receive is 1,800 ÷ 1.001, about 1,798.21 MUSD. The form shows this as *Minimum loan*.

## How it works exactly

### The call

```solidity title="BorrowerOperations (mezo-org/musd)"
function openTrove(uint256 _debtAmount, address _upperHint, address _lowerHint) external payable;
```

`msg.value` is the BTC to lock (18 decimals, BTC is Mezo's native asset). `_debtAmount` is the MUSD you receive. The
call is sent from your wallet directly to BorrowerOperations; no MEZONK contract is involved.

### What Mezo adds to the debt

```
fee          = debtAmount × borrowingRate / 1e18      (0 in recovery mode)
netDebt      = debtAmount + fee                        must be ≥ minNetDebt
compositeDebt = netDebt + MUSD_GAS_COMPENSATION        (200 MUSD)
ICR          = BTC × price / compositeDebt
```

The 200 MUSD gas compensation is minted to Mezo's gas pool, not to you. It pays whoever liquidates the loan if that
ever happens, and is burned back when you close the loan, which is why closing costs 200 MUSD less than the debt shown.

### The checks, in the order the app runs them

The app mirrors BorrowerOperations in `checkOpen` (`apps/web/src/lib/mezo/borrow.ts`), so it can explain a revert
before you sign:

| Check | Normal mode | Recovery mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net debt (amount + fee) ≥ `minNetDebt` | yes | yes (fee is 0) |
| Ratio of the new loan | ≥ `MCR` (110%) | ≥ `CCR` (150%) |
| System ratio after opening | ≥ `CCR` (checked by Mezo) | not applicable |
| Wallet has no active loan | yes | yes |

Mezo also records a *max borrowing capacity* when the loan opens: the debt that would put this loan at 110% at the
opening price. Later borrowing on the same loan can never exceed it, even if BTC rises.

### Right before the wallet prompt

The numbers on screen can be up to 30 seconds old, so `prepareOpen` repeats the work at the latest block:

1. Reads price, fee, minimums, MCR, CCR, interest rate, system collateral and debt at one block number, then
   `checkRecoveryMode(price)` at the same block.
2. Re-runs `checkOpen`. If the loan now fails a check, it stops with the reason.
3. If BTC moved and the ratio fell more than 2% (relative) below what you saw, it stops and asks you to review.
4. Finds hints (below), then estimates gas and sends it explicitly with a 25% margin, so the wallet does not
   estimate against a lagging node.

### Hints

Mezo keeps loans in a list sorted by nominal ratio (`NICR = BTC × 1e20 / principal`, no price). Inserting a loan in
the right place is cheaper if you pass its neighbours. The app computes the new loan's NICR, calls
`HintHelpers.getApproxHint(NICR, trials, seed)` with `trials = max(15, 15 × √listSize)`, then
`SortedTroves.findInsertPosition(NICR, hint, hint)`. A stale hint only costs gas, because Mezo re-checks it. If the
lookup fails, the app sends zero hints and Mezo walks the list itself.

### Recovery mode

Mezo is in recovery mode when the whole system's ratio (TCR) is under 150%. In that mode Mezo only allows changes
that make loans safer. For opening a loan, that means:

- no borrowing fee,
- the new loan must be at 150% or more, not 110%.

The app reads `checkRecoveryMode` every time and shows a notice when it's on.

### Liquidation and redemption

- **Liquidation:** if your loan's ratio falls below 110%, anyone can liquidate it on Mezo. The loan is closed and the
  locked BTC goes to cover the debt.
- **Redemption:** anyone can hand Mezo MUSD and take BTC at face value from the loans with the lowest ratios first.
  Your debt drops by the same dollar value, so you lose nothing in dollars, but you end up holding less BTC. A higher
  ratio makes it less likely to be your loan.

### Errors you might see

The app turns Mezo's revert strings into plain messages (`explainBorrowRevert`):

| Mezo says | The app says |
| --- | --- |
| `PriceFeed: Oracle is stale.` | Mezo's BTC price feed didn't answer in time. Try again in a moment. |
| `ICR < MCR`, `TCR < CCR` | The BTC price moved and this loan would be under Mezo's minimum ratio. |
| `net debt must be greater than minimum` | That's under Mezo's minimum loan size. |
| `Trove is active` | This wallet already has a Mezo loan. Borrow more on it instead. |

The stale-oracle case is usually Mezo testnet's public RPC: it load-balances over a node that can lag far behind,
and reads served by it see a price older than 60 seconds. The app retries those reads automatically.
