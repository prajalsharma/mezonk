MEZONK connects your Mezo loan to the three places where money moves: the trade ticket, the claim button and your
portfolio. If a buy needs more MUSD than your wallet holds, the ticket offers to borrow exactly the difference. When
you win, the claim can repay the loan with the winnings. Your portfolio shows the loan next to your bets, with what
would happen to it if they win.

Follow the loop from left to right: BTC goes into a Mezo loan, MUSD comes out, it backs a trade, the winnings come
back as MUSD and pay the loan down, and the BTC is unlocked.

## From the trade ticket

When you're buying and your MUSD balance is below what the ticket needs (cost plus fee, plus anything you're placing
on the book), a box appears under the ticket.

**If you already have a Mezo loan,** it says *You're … MUSD short. Borrow it from your Mezo loan?* and shows:

- *Ratio*, before and after,
- *Loan-to-value*, before and after (what you owe divided by your BTC's value, so 50% at a 200% ratio),
- *One-time fee*.

Press *Borrow … MUSD* and sign once. When it confirms, the MUSD is in your wallet and you place the trade as usual. If
the new ratio would be under 150%, the box warns you and suggests adding BTC first. If Mezo wouldn't allow it (over
the loan's limit, under 110%, recovery mode), the box says why and links to *Manage your loan*.

**If you don't have a loan yet,** it says how much BTC a loan for the shortfall would lock at 200%, and links to
*Open a loan for this trade →*. That opens the *Borrow* page with the amount filled in. After the loan confirms, a
*Back to your trade* button returns you to the market.

The amount borrowed is the shortfall rounded up to the next cent. A new loan can't be smaller than Mezo's minimum,
so if the shortfall is smaller, the form fills in the minimum and the rest stays in your wallet.

## From a claim

When a market you hold shares in is final and you have a Mezo loan, the claim button has a checkbox: *Use winnings
to repay my Mezo loan (… MUSD)*. If you tick it:

1. You sign the claim. The exchange pays your winnings to your wallet.
2. When that confirms, your wallet asks for a second signature: a `repayMUSD` to Mezo for the smaller of the payout and
   the most your loan can be repaid while staying open.

If the second step fails or you reject it, the claim still went through and the MUSD is in your wallet. The app says
so and links to the *Borrow* page to repay later. If your loan is already at Mezo's minimum size, the checkbox is
replaced by a note: winnings can only pay it off by closing the loan, which you do on the *Borrow* page.

## On your portfolio

*Your Mezo loan* sits above your positions with *BTC locked*, *You owe*, *Ratio* and *Liquidated if BTC falls to*,
and one line of projection:

- If the best case would cover the loan: *If your open bets win, the … MUSD they pay covers the whole loan: close it
  and unlock … BTC.*
- Otherwise: what your debt and ratio would be after repaying the most Mezo allows from it.

It is labelled as a best case: *losing bets pay nothing*. The best case adds, for every open position, the larger of
your Yes and No shares (each pays 1 MUSD if it wins), plus anything you can already claim.

## How it works exactly

### Borrowing the shortfall

```
need      = notional + fee + restCost        (the buy as quoted, plus any rest placed on the book)
shortfall = need − musdBalance
amount    = shortfall rounded up to 0.01 MUSD
```

With an open loan the ticket calls `prepareDraw` and then `BorrowerOperations.withdrawMUSD(amount, upper, lower)`
from your wallet, one signature. It runs the same checks as the *Borrow* tab (capacity, 110%, recovery mode) and uses
the ratio you saw minus 2% as a floor. Without a loan it links to `/borrow?need=<amount>&return=/markets/<id>`. The
`return` value is only accepted if it matches `/markets/<number>`, so the link can't send you anywhere else.

Borrowing and buying are two transactions, not one. MEZONK has no contract that touches Mezo loans, so it can't
borrow on your behalf; your wallet has to call Mezo itself.

### Why repaying from winnings takes two signatures

Mezo's `BorrowerOperations` burns repaid MUSD from the address that calls it. That is true of `repayMUSD`, and also of
`repayMUSDWithSignature` (in `BorrowerOperationsSignatures`): it checks the borrower's signature but passes
`msg.sender` as the account to burn from. So a single transaction that claims and repays would need a contract to
receive your winnings first and then repay from its own balance. MEZONK chose not to add a contract that holds user
winnings, even briefly, so the claim pays your wallet and your wallet repays.

The second step re-reads your MUSD balance from the chain after the claim and repays

```
repayAmount = min(claimPayout, maxRepay)      maxRepay = debt − 200 MUSD − minNetDebt
```

never more than the claim paid.

### The projection

```
best case = Σ over unfinalized positions of max(yesShares, noShares)  +  claimable now
repayable = min(best case, maxRepay)
```

If the best case reaches `closeCost = debt − 200 MUSD`, the card says the loan can be closed. Nothing on the card moves
money.
