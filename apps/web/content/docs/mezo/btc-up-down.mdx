`MezonkBtcRounds` is live on Mezo Mainnet at
[`0xc27f434195e4aF70782817A280A5D3Cb5de2ECa8`](https://explorer.mezo.org/address/0xc27f434195e4aF70782817A280A5D3Cb5de2ECa8).
Bet at [/btc](/btc).

The Mezo team asked for exactly this: a way to bet on BTC going up or down, using BTC. The rest of MEZONK settles in
MUSD, Mezo's bitcoin-backed dollar. This part doesn't. You stake native BTC on *Up* or *Down* for the next round, the
price is read from the same Mezo PriceFeed that prices Mezo's loans, and if you're right you're paid in BTC from the
stakes of the people who were wrong. Nothing is converted, nothing is wrapped, and there is no house: the contract
holds nothing but stakes and pays out nothing but stakes.

## The plain version

- **A round is an hour.** Rounds run back to back on a fixed interval (one hour on Mezo Mainnet). Each round
  has a betting window (one interval) followed by a price window (the next interval). At the start of the price
  window the BTC price is captured; at its end it's captured again.
- **You pick a side and a size.** During the betting window you stake BTC on *Up* or *Down*. The size is chosen in
  dollars (the page offers $10, $25, $50 and $100 chips) and converted to BTC at Mezo's price at that moment. The
  contract checks the dollar value itself and refuses stakes outside its limits.
- **Up wins if the close price is above the lock price.** Down wins if it's below. Winners share the whole pool, both
  sides' stakes, in proportion to how much each of them staked, minus a 1% fee.
- **Anything that isn't a clean result is a refund.** A tie, a round where nobody backed the winning side, nobody
  backed the losing side, or a round where the price wasn't captured in time: everyone gets their exact stake back and
  no fee is taken.
- **You claim when you like.** Winnings and refunds are paid in BTC when you press *Claim*, for one round or many at
  once. Claims never expire.

### A worked example

These are example numbers, not live data. Say Mezo Mainnet's settings apply (1% fee, stakes between $8 and $125) and
Mezo's PriceFeed says BTC is $80,000 when round 12's betting window opens.

| Bettor | Side | Stake | In BTC at $80,000 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alice | Up | $100 | 0.00125 BTC |
| Bob | Up | $50 | 0.000625 BTC |
| Carol | Down | $100 | 0.00125 BTC |

The pool is 0.003125 BTC. At the lock the price is captured at $80,000. An hour later it's captured again at $80,400.
Up wins.

- Fee: 1% of the whole pool, 0.00003125 BTC. The remaining 0.00309375 BTC goes to the Up side.
- Alice staked 0.00125 of the 0.001875 BTC on Up, two thirds, so she gets 0.0020625 BTC. At $80,400 that's about
  $165.83 for a $100 stake.
- Bob staked one third of the Up side, so he gets 0.00103125 BTC, about $82.91 for his $50.
- Carol gets nothing. Her 0.00125 BTC is what Alice and Bob won.

Alice's payout plus Bob's plus the fee is exactly the pool: 0.0020625 + 0.00103125 + 0.00003125 = 0.003125 BTC.

If the second capture had been $79,600 instead, Down wins. Carol is the only Down bettor, so she gets the whole pool
less the fee: 0.00309375 BTC, about $246.26 at the new price, for her $100. If it had been exactly $80,000 again,
nobody wins: all three get their exact BTC back and no fee is taken.

## How it works exactly

### Round timing

The contract has four immutable timing values set at deployment: `genesis` (a unix time at or after deployment, the
deploy script rounds it up to the next whole hour), `interval`, `captureWindow` and the PriceFeed address. From those,
for round `r` (counting from 0):

```solidity title="MezonkBtcRounds.sol"
betStart(r)  = genesis + r * interval
lockTime(r)  = genesis + (r + 1) * interval   // betting closes, first price capture
closeTime(r) = genesis + (r + 2) * interval   // second price capture
currentRound() = (block.timestamp - genesis) / interval
```

Round `r`'s price window is round `r + 1`'s betting window, so exactly one round is open for bets at any time. The
constructor requires `interval ≥ 5 minutes`, `60 s ≤ captureWindow < interval` (so a capture can never be mistaken for
the next boundary's) and `genesis ≥ now`. Mezo Mainnet uses one hour and ten minutes
(`contracts/script/DeployBtcRounds.s.sol` defaults).

### Placing a bet

```solidity title="MezonkBtcRounds.sol"
function bet(uint256 round, Side side) external payable;
```

In order, it:

1. Reverts `IsPaused` if the owner has paused betting.
2. Reverts `BettingClosed` unless `round` is `currentRound()` and the lock time hasn't passed.
3. Calls `priceFeed.fetchPrice()`. This is Mezo's own check: it reverts `PriceFeed: Oracle is stale.` if the oracle's
   last update is more than 60 seconds old, so a bet can never be sized at an old price.
4. Computes `usd = msg.value × price / 1e18` and reverts `StakeOutsideRange(usd)` unless
   `minStakeUsd ≤ usd ≤ maxStakeUsd`. The limits are in USD so the chips mean the same thing whatever BTC costs.
5. Reverts `OtherSide` if you already have a stake on the other side in this round. Staking again on the same side
   adds to it.
6. Adds `msg.value` to the round's `upPool` or `downPool`, records your `amount` and the `usd` it was worth (for
   display only), and emits `BetPlaced(round, account, side, amount, usd, price)`.

The page sizes a chip with `btcForUsd(usd)`, which returns the BTC that `usd` buys at the price right now and the
price it used. BTC can move between that quote and your transaction confirming, which is why the limits are
$8 to $125 rather than exactly $10 to $100: the four chips fit with room for the price to drift either way.

### One price per boundary

Mezo's oracle reports only the latest price; there is no history to look up later (see
[BTC price markets](/docs/mezo/btc-price-markets) for how that was verified). So someone has to read the price at
each boundary, while it's current. That someone is a keeper, and anyone can be one:

```solidity title="MezonkBtcRounds.sol"
function roll(uint256 round) external;
```

`roll(r)` may be called from `lockTime(r)` until `captureWindow` seconds later (`NotYet` before, `WindowClosed`
after, `AlreadyRolled` if done). It reads `fetchPrice()` and the oracle's `latestRoundData()` and requires a positive
answer whose `updatedAt` is at or after `lockTime(r)`, so the price always describes that boundary and never a moment
before it (a too-old update reverts `NotYet`, and the keeper simply tries again inside the window). Then, in one
call, it:

- locks round `r` with that price as its `lockPrice`, and
- if round `r − 1` was locked and not yet settled, settles it with the same price as its `closePrice`, computes its
  result, and if the result is Up or Down takes the fee from the pool (`pool × feeBps / 10,000`) into `fees`.

It emits `Rolled(round, price, updatedAt, settledRound, settledResult)`. One capture closes one round and locks the
next, so a round's close price is by construction the next round's lock price. There is no gap between rounds for a
price to slip through, and the keeper has exactly one job per interval.

### Deciding a round

`result(round)` returns `Pending`, `Up`, `Down` or `Void`:

| Case | Result | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `closePrice > lockPrice`, both sides staked | Up | The Up side wins the Down side's stakes |
| `closePrice < lockPrice`, both sides staked | Down | The Down side wins the Up side's stakes |
| `closePrice == lockPrice` | Void | Nobody was right |
| One side has no stakes | Void | There's nothing to win; paying "winners" from the pool would hand them their own money minus a fee |
| Lock capture missed (`now > lockTime + captureWindow`, never locked) | Void | No honest lock price exists, and the contract won't guess |
| Close capture missed (`now > closeTime + captureWindow`, locked, never settled) | Void | Same, for the close price |

A void round refunds every stake in full and takes no fee: `fee` stays 0 for that round, and nothing is added to
`fees`. A later roll never touches a round whose window has passed, so a void result is final.

### Payouts and claims

For a settled round, your payout is:

```text
Void:            your stake
Losing side:     0
Winning side:    your stake × (upPool + downPool − fee) / winningPool
```

Integer division leaves at most 1 wei per winner in the contract; the fuzz test `testFuzz_poolIsConserved` checks
that payouts plus the fee never exceed the pool and fall short of it by at most a few wei.

```solidity title="MezonkBtcRounds.sol"
function claim(uint256[] calldata roundIds) external returns (uint256 total);
function claimable(uint256 round, address account) external view returns (uint256);
```

`claim` walks the rounds you pass, skips any where you have no stake, already claimed, or the result is still
`Pending`, marks the rest claimed, and sends the total as native BTC in one transfer. It reverts `NothingToClaim` if
the total is zero, and it's reentrancy-guarded. **Claims never expire**: a round that settled months ago still pays
when you claim it, and nothing in the contract can take an unclaimed payout.

### What the owner can and can't do

| Function | Bounds | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `setStakeLimits(minUsd, maxUsd)` | `minUsd > 0`, `maxUsd ≥ minUsd` | New bets only |
| `setFee(bps)` | at most 500 (5%) | Rounds settled after the change |
| `setPaused(bool)` | | Stops new bets. Rolling and claiming always work, so money never gets stuck |
| `sweep(to)` | `fees > 0` | Moves accrued fees out. Stakes can't be swept: `fees` is the only amount this ever pays |

Ownership is two-step (`Ownable2Step`), so it can't be handed to a wrong address by mistake. There is no function that
moves a stake anywhere except back to the wallet that placed it, and no upgrade mechanism. Events: `StakeLimitsSet`,
`FeeSet`, `PausedSet`, `FeesSwept`.

### Limits

- One side per wallet per round. You can add to a stake, but not hedge by staking the other side too.
- Stakes are checked in USD at Mezo's price at that moment, so the same chip buys a different amount of BTC an hour
  later.
- If Mezo's oracle is stale (no update in 60 seconds), `bet` reverts. The page should show this as "price
  unavailable, try again", not as an error you caused.
- You can only bet on the round that is open now, and only until its lock time. There's no betting on later rounds
  in advance.

### What can go wrong

- **The keeper misses a window.** The market-maker bot rolls every round (with `MM_BTC_ROUNDS` set), but anyone can
  call `roll`. If nobody does within the capture window, or the oracle is stale for the whole window, the rounds that
  needed that price are void: you get your stake back, no fee. You don't lose money, but you also don't win, and your
  BTC sits in the contract until you claim it.
- **The fee.** 1% of the whole pool on Mezo Mainnet, taken only on rounds with a winner. The owner can raise
  it to at most 5%; `feeBps()` is public and the page should show the current value.
- **BTC price risk on the stake itself.** You stake BTC and you're paid BTC. If you win *Down*, you receive more BTC,
  but each one is worth less than when you staked. If a round is void, you get the same BTC back, now worth whatever
  BTC is worth. Your result in dollars is the BTC result and BTC's move combined.
- **Price capture timing.** A capture is the first oracle update at or after the boundary, read within the window,
  so "the price at 14:00" means the price a little after 14:00, usually within a minute or two. Both captures follow
  the same rule.
- **Not audited.** This contract has not been reviewed by an independent firm. It's about 300 lines, standalone, and
  tested (`contracts/test/exchange/BtcRounds.t.sol`), and the only money it holds is stakes, but read it before you
  stake more than you can afford to lose.

### What reviewers should notice

- **Native BTC stays BTC.** Stakes are `msg.value`; payouts are native transfers. No wrapped token, no MUSD
  conversion, no swap, no approval step.
- **Same price Mezo uses.** The only price source is Mezo's MUSD PriceFeed, the one that prices Mezo's loans and
  liquidations (`0xc5aC5A8892230E0A3e1c473881A2de7353fFcA88` on Mezo Mainnet). MEZONK adds no oracle of its own, and the
  contract refuses stale or pre-boundary prices the same way the [price resolver](/docs/mezo/btc-price-markets) does.
- **No counterparty.** Winners are paid from losers. The contract never fronts a payout, never holds an inventory,
  and can never owe more than it holds: a round pays out exactly its own pool less the fee, or exactly its own pool.
- **Standalone.** It doesn't touch the exchange, the factory or MUSD, so it can be deployed and paused on its own.
- **A refund is the default.** Every unclear case (tie, one-sided, missed capture) resolves to "everyone gets their
  BTC back, no fee", not to a judgment call.
